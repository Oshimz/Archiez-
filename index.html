What is parental alienation?

Cafcass describes it as a child's unjustified hostility toward one parent, resulting from the other parent's psychological manipulation. It is not a legal or medical diagnosis in the UK.

What happens to the children

Advocates for alienated parents argue that children who lose a relationship with a loving parent can suffer long-term harm.

  • Distress, anxiety and low self-esteem
  • Difficulty trusting in later relationships
  • Loss of extended family, such as grandparents
  • Being pulled into adult conflict

How fathers say the system encourages it

Many fathers' groups make these arguments:

Long delays in family courts let the child's bond fade.
Contact orders are rarely enforced when ignored.
The parent who has the child day to day holds most of the power.
Benefits and maintenance rules may give no incentive to share care.
Professionals often lack training to spot manipulation early.

Other perspectives

This topic is contested. Some researchers argue that alienation claims are sometimes used by abusive parents to discredit a protective parent, and that a child's reluctance can have real causes. The President of the Family Court has called the "alienation" label unhelpful. Courts must examine why a child resists contact, and many fathers and mothers have been affected on both sides.
